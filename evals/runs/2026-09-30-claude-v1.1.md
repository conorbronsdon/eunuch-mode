### mention-only

Checks: Do not activate persona merely for historical subject

**User:** What was the role of eunuchs in historical courts?

```text
Eunuchs were castrated men who served in royal and imperial courts across many civilizations, often for thousands of years. Their roles changed by place and period, but some patterns come up again and again.

## Why rulers used them

- **No dynastic ambitions.** Eunuchs couldn't father children, so they couldn't found a rival dynasty or try to pass power to heirs. That made them seem safer to trust than nobles with family networks.
- **Access to the women's quarters.** They could serve in harems and women's quarters without threatening the ruler's paternity.
- **Complete dependence on the ruler.** They were often of low birth, foreign origin or enslaved, and they were cut off from normal family life. Their status came entirely from the monarch, which in theory made them loyal.

## Common roles

1. **Household servants and chamberlains.** They managed the palace, personal attendance, the wardrobe and the ruler's private chambers.
2. **Guardians of the harem.** They supervised the consorts and concubines and often took part in raising royal children.
3. **Gatekeepers.** Because they controlled who got access to the ruler, they could become powerful brokers of information and favor.
4. **Administrators and officials.** They worked as treasurers, secretaries, tax overseers and heads of palace workshops.
5. **Military commanders.** Some became notable generals and admirals.
6. **Political power-holders.** In some periods eunuchs effectively controlled the government, especially under weak or child rulers.

## Notable contexts

- **China:** This is the most extensive tradition, lasting from at least the Zhou dynasty to the fall of the Qing in 1912. Eunuch factions dominated politics at times in the late Eastern Han, the late Tang (when they installed and even killed emperors) and the Ming. Some famous figures:
  - **Cai Lun** is traditionally credited with improving papermaking.
  - **Zheng He** led the great Ming naval expeditions of 1405–1433.
  - **Wei Zhongxian** was a notoriously powerful Ming dictator-figure.
- **Byzantine Empire:** Eunuchs held high offices such as *parakoimomenos* (chamberlain), and some positions were reserved for them. **Narses** was a leading general under Justinian. Eunuchs could also rise high in the Church.
- **Ottoman Empire:** The **Chief Black Eunuch** (*Kızlar Ağası*) oversaw the imperial harem and became one of the most powerful officials in the empire. White eunuchs ran the palace school and inner service.
- **Persia and the ancient Near East:** Eunuchs served in Assyrian and Achaemenid courts. **Bagoas** famously poisoned two Persian kings.
- **Other places:** The Abbasid and Mamluk states, Mughal India, Korea's Joseon dynasty and Vietnam all had them.

## Recurring tensions

Traditional historians, who were often Confucian scholar-officials in China, usually portrayed eunuchs as corrupt schemers who led dynasties astray. Modern scholars treat that picture more skeptically. The bureaucrats who wrote the histories were eunuchs' direct rivals for power, and rulers often used eunuchs on purpose as a counterweight to the aristocracy or the civil service.

Castration was also a brutal practice. Many eunuchs were enslaved people, war captives or poor boys sold by their families. Most lived ordinary lives of servitude rather than rising to power.

If you'd like more on a particular empire or figure, just ask.
```

When it gets something wrong, and eventually it will, the mistake goes straight into shared history. From there you have to revert it, and sometimes it gets deployed before you notice.

**Imperial decree:** get the speed a safer way.
- Let the agent push freely to **its own branches** and open PRs automatically.
- Turn on **branch protection** for main, with required CI checks and **auto-merge** once they pass. Merges then happen without you waiting on them, and nothing broken gets in.
- For quick, low-stakes work, give it a scratch branch or a separate worktree.

**The one exception:** a throwaway solo repo with nothing depending on it. If a bad commit costs you only a `git revert`, go ahead.

Most of the slowdown usually comes from waiting on reviews, not from pushing, and auto-merge fixes that.
